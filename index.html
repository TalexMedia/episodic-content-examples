Episodic Branded Content

Eleven brands that invested in creating series and the results they generated.

Here are eleven shows worth taking inspiration from. Eleven brands that invested in making shows that are audience-first, and people have chosen to watch them because of their entertainment value alone.

None of them feel like advertisements, because they were produced just like regular TV.

Made for the audience

Every show here was made for the people watching first, not for the brand.

Free to watch

Each show is worth your time whether or not you ever buy anything from the business.

Built to come back to

One format repeats episode after episode, so watching becomes a habit.

Take a look through the seven long form and four short form examples below, and think about what your own version of a series might look like.

02 · Special Case

A Special Case: Red Bull

One brand built its own media company around the idea and has run it for nearly twenty years.

Red Bull had been selling an energy drink for twenty years when the company opened Red Bull Media House in 2007. From then on Red Bull made its own films, series and live events.

The strategy was to own the events and the footage rather than sponsor somebody else's, which is the approach Talex calls Legendeering. A sponsor pays to appear at another company's event, and when the event ends the footage belongs to whoever shot it. Red Bull built its own events instead, so the film library kept growing and stayed in house. Red Bull says it now runs more than 1,250 events a year in over 160 countries, with more than 700 athletes across more than 100 disciplines.

Red Bull Media House describes its own mission as inspiring people with "beyond the ordinary" stories. The films follow athletes attempting things most of us never would. Red Bull's bet was that the person who wants to watch that kind of film is the same person who buys the drink.

Red Bull Media House also produces and licenses live broadcast events for other broadcasters, so part of the output earns revenue rather than only costing money.

Red Bull bought the Jaguar Formula One team from Ford in late 2004. That team has won six constructors' titles and eight drivers' titles through the 2025 season. In October 2012 Red Bull sent Felix Baumgartner to the edge of space and streamed the jump live. More than eight million people watched at once, a live record for YouTube at the time.

What They Have In Common

  • A format lasts longer than any single film. You can describe each show above in one sentence: a junk car made to run again, one region and one local chef, two people who fell out doing each other's makeup.
  • The brand can play any part in the story. It can be the reason the episode exists, as with the eBay builds, or barely appear at all, as with the YETI films.
  • A show that returns gives people a reason to come back. A single film gets one launch, while a format gets a new launch every time it returns, and the back catalog keeps earning views in between.
  • Long and short both work. Huckberry's episodes run about half an hour, Volvo's films run about a minute, and both are returning series. Pick the length the idea needs.
  • Small productions work too. One e.l.f. episode is two people doing each other's makeup at a table, and it drew more than two million views on YouTube by itself.
