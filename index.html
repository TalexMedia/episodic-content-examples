Episodic Branded Content

Nine shows a brand paid for and an audience chose to watch, the results each one got, and one that shows what happens without a format.

Most brand video gets made once, published, and forgotten. Each show below works differently, because it is a format rather than a film: one repeatable idea with a new subject every time, so people know what they are getting and come back for the next one.

Two scales, kept apart. Six of these are long form, built to hold someone for eight minutes or more, and three are short form, built around a single minute. They are different jobs and their view counts are not comparable, so they sit in separate sections and every card says which it is.

On the numbers. View counts, episode counts and channel figures were pulled from the YouTube API on 12 August 2026, so read them as a snapshot. Where a figure comes from a brand's or an agency's own award entry, the card says so.

What They Have In Common

  • The format outlasts any single film. Each show above has one sentence you can repeat: a junk car brought back to life, one region and one local chef, two people who fell out doing each other's makeup.
  • The brand can sit anywhere in the story. It can cause the whole episode, as with the eBay builds, or barely appear at all, as with the YETI films.
  • Repetition is what builds the habit. A single film gets one launch, while a format gets a new launch every time it returns, and the back catalog keeps earning views in between.
  • Length is a choice, not a measure of quality. Huckberry holds people for half an hour and Volvo does its work in about a minute, and both were built to return. Pick the length the idea needs, then judge it against others of that length.
  • It works at almost any budget. These run from a crew of four up to a stunt with two moving trucks, and two people at a table drew two million views on their own.
