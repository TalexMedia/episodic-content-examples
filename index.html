Episodic Branded Content

Ten brands that invested in episodic video, and what they got back.

Here are ten shows worth taking inspiration from. Each one comes from a brand that put real money into episodic video. They hold up on their own, because people watch them for their own reasons.

Made for the audience

Every one of these was built for the people watching, not for the brand paying for it.

Free to watch

Each one is worth your time whether or not you ever buy a thing from the brand behind it.

Built to come back to

One idea comes back episode after episode, so watching turns into a habit.

Take a look through them, seven long form and three short form, and think about what your own version would look like.

What They Have In Common

  • A format lasts longer than any single film. You can describe each show above in one sentence: a junk car made to run again, one region and one local chef, two people who fell out doing each other's makeup.
  • The brand can play any part in the story. It can be the reason the episode exists, as with the eBay builds, or barely appear at all, as with the YETI films.
  • A show that returns gives people a reason to come back. A single film gets one launch, while a format gets a new launch every time it returns, and the back catalog keeps earning views in between.
  • Long and short both work. Huckberry's episodes run about half an hour, Volvo's films run about a minute, and both are returning series. Pick the length the idea needs.
  • Small productions work too. One e.l.f. episode is two people doing each other's makeup at a table, and it drew more than two million views on YouTube by itself.
